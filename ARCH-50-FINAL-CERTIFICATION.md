## 1. What was built

**One egress gate for everything that leaves the process; a sovereign edition an operator can run with nothing leaving at all; the commercial machinery to sell it (annual and INR prices, promo codes, invoiced contracts, revenue metrics); and disaster recovery that is measured, not promised.**

**The egress gate** (`app/core/egress.py`). Fourteen channels. Seven carry a tenant's data to a destination the tenant (or, for AI providers, the platform) chose and are what an organization's lockdown governs: outgoing webhooks, warehouse & BI egress, ERP over HTTP, ERP over SFTP, SSO metadata (OIDC / SAML), the tenant's own SMTP server, AI model providers (platform keys and BYOK). Seven belong to the operator: the platform email relay, the operator's local model, object storage, the payment gateway, internal services (the reranker), DNS resolvers (domain verification) and the off-host backup mirror; two of them (the local model and internal services) may reach ONLY the operator's configured host, in every mode. Every outbound client asks the gate BEFORE a name is resolved: the SSRF-safe HTTP client is constructed only by the gate (`GuardedHTTPClient`, a subclass that decides before connecting), every provider SDK and `google-auth` is handed a gated transport, SMTP asks before `validate_smtp_host`, SFTP before `_resolve`, boto3 clients get an attached gate hook, Stripe asks before each use, and DNS verification asks for each resolver. The decision has two layers: the deployment (`EGRESS_MODE=open|deny`; any other value is deny) and the organization's lockdown (Enterprise; allow rules by host, `*.suffix`, IP or CIDR, per channel and port; normalized; `*`, URLs, credentials and 0.0.0.0/0 refused; at most 200). A refusal raises `EgressDenied` (403 `EGRESS_DENIED` at the API; a webhook delivery goes DEAD without retries; an ERP posting fails PERMANENT, never "uncertain"; Dodo never retries it), is counted in ONE row per organization, channel, destination, reason and hour (an `INSERT … ON CONFLICT` upsert, safe under concurrency), and audited once per row in the organization's audit log (a refusal made on no organization's behalf is counted, not audited). Both job loops attribute every connection to the job's organization, so a lockdown applies to background work too. **A lockdown outlives a downgrade:** editing it needs the capability, enforcing it does not; only the operator lifts it (`scripts/egress_admin.py clear --organization … --reason …`, audited, the rules kept).

**The sovereign edition** (`FLOWPILOT_EDITION=sovereign`). *Deny mode* (`EGRESS_MODE=deny`): nothing leaves except the hosts the operator's own settings name, each for its own channel (`S3_ENDPOINT_URL`, `LOCAL_LLM_BASE_URL`, `RERANKER_URL`, `PLATFORM_SMTP_HOST`, `DNS_RESOLVERS`, `FLOWPILOT_BACKUP_S3_ENDPOINT`), plus `EGRESS_OPERATOR_HOSTS` (host, `*.suffix`, IP or CIDR, optionally `CHANNEL=` scoped and port-pinned); tenant allow rules can narrow that set, never widen it. *The operator's local model* (llama.cpp server, vLLM or Ollama behind an OpenAI-compatible URL, through the pinned `openai` SDK): `LOCAL_LLM_MODE=off|fallback|exclusive`; `exclusive` serves every completion and stream whatever a tenant selected; `fallback` serves a call only after the configured provider failed or the gate refused it (an SDK-wrapped refusal is unwrapped: `egress.refusal_in`); `off` reports a refusal as 403, never as an outage. *The licence*: `flowpilot-licence/1`, Ed25519 over canonical JSON, verified offline on every read against public keys pinned in `app/core/licence_keys.py` (empty in the repository; a development key is refused when `ENVIRONMENT=production`); creating an organization on a sovereign deployment needs a usable licence with room (402 `LICENCE_REQUIRED`); existing organizations never stop. `scripts/licence_tool.py` makes keys and licences. *The release manifest*: a CycloneDX 1.5 JSON SBOM (every pinned Python requirement and every npm package in the lockfile, with purls, declared licences and the lockfile's integrity hashes), `SHA256SUMS` over the release and an Ed25519 signature of it (`scripts/release_manifest.py build|verify`) — JSON only, no third XML module.

**RevOps.** *Plan price books*: DRAFT → PUBLISHED (the database refuses any change to a published book or its entries; its digest is stored) → RETIRED; one published book per currency (USD and INR); monthly and annual prices; a gateway price names exactly one (tier, interval, currency) across every book and tier version (a trigger). *Promo codes*: percent or amount, once / repeating / forever, a cap, a deadline, tier and currency scope; quoted without writing; reserved at checkout under the code's row lock (N concurrent checkouts cannot over-allocate), redeemed when the subscription or contract actually starts, released or expired otherwise; one live redemption per organization; a code without a gateway coupon applies to invoiced contracts only. *Checkout* prices from the books (interval, currency, seats, promo), records what it sold, and answers 503 `BILLING_GATEWAY_UNAVAILABLE` (never 500) when the gateway is down, keeping nothing. *Invoiced enterprise contracts* (how Enterprise and every sovereign deployment is sold): term, seats, an amount per month / quarter / year in USD or INR, tax, payment terms, PO; activate (assigns the plan, redeems the promo; refused while a gateway subscription or another contract is live), one invoice per period (idempotent under racing issuers; the short last period prorated by days; discounts by the promo's duration; tax on the discounted subtotal), pay / void (a voided period re-issues) / end. *Revenue*: MRR and ARR per currency (never summed across currencies), movements (new, expansion, contraction, churn), receivables, and a snapshot of each closed month frozen by the daily `revops.sweep` (LIGHT profile, cron `flowpilot-sweep revops`).

**Disaster recovery, measured.** Point-in-time recovery with PostgreSQL's own `archive_command`, `pg_basebackup` and `restore_command`, driven by `scripts/dr_pitr.py` (`archive-wal` writes a temporary name, fsyncs and renames, treats an identical segment as success and REFUSES a different one under the same name). A heartbeat row a minute on the primary makes the loss of a restore-to-latest a measured number; every drill (PITR, logical RESTORE, CHAOS) lands in `dr_drills` and the operator console compares the latest of each with RPO 300 s / RTO 3600 s. Cron: heartbeat every minute, base backup nightly, a PITR drill weekly; the ARCH-41 weekly restore drill now records itself (`restore-drill --record`).

**Consoles.** Organization › *Egress lockdown* (Enterprise; OWNER switches and edits, ADMIN reads, tests and sees refusals). Billing: annual / monthly, USD / INR, seats and a promo code on the plan selector, and the organization's invoiced contract with its invoices. Platform admin › *Sovereign edition* (Overview, Egress — mode, declared hosts, every refusal, a test —, Local model with health, Licence, Release, Recovery) and *Revenue operations* (Revenue, Price books, Promo codes, Contracts).

**Data.** Fourteen tables: `egress_policies`, `egress_allow_rules`, `egress_refusals`, `platform_licences`, `dr_heartbeats`, `dr_drills`, `plan_price_books`, `plan_price_book_entries`, `promo_codes`, `promo_redemptions`, `checkout_selections`, `enterprise_contracts`, `contract_invoices`, `revenue_snapshots`; three trigger functions (the published-book guard, the entries guard, one gateway price one plan); CHECKs for every closed vocabulary (proven equal to the services' by T2b), the refusal bucket, a measured PASSED PITR drill, invoice arithmetic, ARR = 12 × MRR. One existing function is WIDENED: the pipeline stage guard (§7). Zero column drift. No outbox change.

## 2. The five decisions the roadmap left open

1. **Where the gate sits.** In one module, `app/core/egress.py`, which every outbound client must go through — not a proxy, not a firewall rule the application cannot see. The proof is T3, an AST walk over all of `app/` plus the backup mirror (784 modules): no socket, `http.client`, `httpx`, `requests`, `urllib`, `smtplib`, `paramiko`, `boto3`, Stripe or DNS resolver is opened anywhere except ten sanctioned openers, each of which must itself call the gate; `SSRFSafeHTTPClient` is constructed only by the gate; every provider SDK and `google-auth` constructor receives a gated transport; and the gate's inventory names an opener for every channel. D5 then drives every channel through its REAL client under a network tap: a refused destination opens no socket and resolves no name.
2. **How deny mode fails, and what still works.** Closed: an unknown mode is deny, an unreadable tenant policy refuses (`POLICY_UNAVAILABLE`), and every refusal is counted per destination and hour — and audited in the organization's log when it was made on an organization's behalf. What keeps working with no route out: everything in-process (OCR, extraction, embeddings, retrieval, the review hub, automations, process intelligence), the operator's local model, the operator's object storage, relay, resolvers, reranker and backup mirror (each declared for its channel only), invoiced contracts (no card gateway), the licence and the release signature (both verified offline), and disaster recovery (a local or mounted archive). What does not: hosted AI providers, card checkout, and any webhook, warehouse, ERP or SSO destination the operator has not declared.
3. **The local model is chosen per deployment and is never tenant-supplied.** It is read from the operator's environment in exactly one place (`local_llm.config()`; T5 walks `app/` for the settings' names); no table, column, schema or route carries its URL or model; "LOCAL" is not a BYOK provider and no AI-settings provider can name it (T5, D6); its channel reaches only the host parsed from `LOCAL_LLM_BASE_URL`, in every mode (T4, D5); the operator's key never reaches a response (D6). It decides nothing on its own: its outputs go to review like any model's.
4. **What "measured RPO/RTO" means in the sandbox.** A real drill, not a calculation: D9 initdb's a scratch primary with `archive_mode = on` and `archive_command` = `dr_pitr.py archive-wal`, takes a `pg_basebackup`, writes committed canary rows, crashes the primary (`pg_ctl -m immediate`), then (a) restores to a chosen past instant and checks that EXACTLY the rows committed by then are present and none after, and (b) restores to the end of the archive and measures the loss. RTO is the wall-clock time from "restore begins" to "the restored cluster answers the verification query"; RPO is the newest committed canary on the primary minus the newest one recovered. In the certification run: 81 canary rows committed across 5 archived WAL segments; the restore to a timestamp held exactly the 61 rows committed by then and none after (1.30 s); the restore to the end of the archive lost 3.25 s of commits — the unarchived tail of the segment in progress at the crash — and took 1.27 s: **RPO 3.25 s against 300 s, RTO 1.27 s against 3600 s**, recorded as a PASSED PITR drill. The logical restore of a fresh backup took 1.38 s and compared 206 tables. It then restores the ARCH-41 encrypted logical backup into a scratch database, compares it table by table and records that too. **What the sandbox cannot measure:** latency to a real off-host archive (the archive directory was local), the RTO of a production-sized database (a base backup of a few MB restores in about a second; RTO grows with the base backup and the WAL to replay), the RPO under real `archive_timeout` and load (the drill's loss is the unarchived tail of the segment in progress; the ceiling in production is `archive_timeout` = 60 s plus the archiver's latency, well inside 300 s), disk or network failure, and any of it on Windows.
5. **How the owner reproduces GA-2 on Windows.** `.\run_arch50.ps1 -GA2` (`GA-2-REPORT.md` §4): it drops and re-creates `flowpilot_ga2` on the server `backend\.env` names, migrates it from nothing to the release head, seeds, runs the 36 suites one after another and writes `backend\evidence\ga2\summary.txt`. The same script ran end to end in the sandbox under PowerShell 7.4.6 on Linux — the certification run (§5) and the GA-2 run itself; script paths use forward slashes, the Python and npm executables are found for either platform, and native stderr is captured without tripping Windows PowerShell 5.1's error handling.

## 7. Found and fixed during the build

- **The database refused seven pipeline transitions the application declares legal (found by GA-2; older than ARCH-50).** Since 2026-09-06 `pipeline_state.STAGE_TRANSITIONS` allowed re-queueing from a working stage and retrying from FAILED / QUOTA_BLOCKED straight into EXTRACTING / ENRICHING, but the trigger kept ARCH-10's table, so those moves failed in PostgreSQL (23514) — a quota-blocked document re-run after its limit was raised, or a failed enrichment retried, broke instead of proceeding. The migration installs the application's exact table (downgrade restores ARCH-10's); D3, MS43, MS44, MD19.
- **Cron sweeps that could not enqueue (found by GA-2; older than ARCH-50).** `sweep_process.py` and `sweep_erp_postings.py` enqueue from cron without the worker's handler registry, and `enqueue()` refuses every job type without it — their own gates had registered the handlers themselves. All three sweeps register first; MS31 and X2 run the real cron entry point.
- **A 429 without rate limit headers (GA-2; older than ARCH-50).** The per-key 429 short-circuits the middleware that adds them; it now carries them itself.
- **Every Groq stream failed (found by D6).** The pinned `groq` SDK has no `stream_options` parameter; passing it raised TypeError before any request. It is now sent only to providers that take it (Groq reports usage in `x_groq.usage` unasked).
- **A refusal wrapped by an SDK looked like an outage (found by D5/D6).** OpenAI, Groq and Anthropic wrap a transport error in their own connection error, so a gate refusal was retried, failed over or reported as 502/503. `egress.refusal_in` unwraps it: fallback mode answers from the local model, otherwise 403 `EGRESS_DENIED`; a BYOK key test reports "refused by the egress gate".
- **Names resolved before the gate decided (found by D5).** SFTP resolved the ERP host and SMTP validated (resolved) the tenant's server before any policy check, leaking a DNS query for a refused destination. Both now ask the gate on the configured name first; an SFTP refusal is PERMANENT, never retried.
- **A 422 rendered as 409 (found by the HTTP gates).** The domain exception handler maps by CLASS, so `RevOpsError` / `EgressPolicyError` with a per-instance status all rendered as the class default; each (code, status) now gets its own subclass.
- **A dead payment gateway answered 500 (found by C4).** It now answers 503 `BILLING_GATEWAY_UNAVAILABLE`; the promo reservation and the sale roll back with the request.
- **Two racing refusals could record the later one as the first (found by X1).** The bucket upsert now keeps `LEAST(first_at)` / `GREATEST(last_at)`.
- **Two hosts running the daily RevOps cron could both enqueue (found by X2).** A platform job has no organization and the idempotency UNIQUE does not cover NULL; the look-up and insert now run under an advisory lock.
- **The weekly restore drill measured nothing the console could show.** `restore_drill.py --record` now writes its backup age (RPO) and restore time (RTO) to `dr_drills`, and the cron line passes it. `dr_pitr.py` quotes the paths it writes into `archive_command` / `restore_command` (a Windows path with a backslash or a quote no longer breaks the configuration).
- **A plan priced only in a price book showed no Subscribe button (found in the browser).** The CTA looked at the tier version's own price; it now asks the price book first.
- **A refreshed page could lose the signed-in user (found in the browser).** Two concurrent session restores raced; the loser cleared the cached user and the winner only set the token, so guards reading the user (the platform shell's superuser check) bounced a superadmin off `/admin/*`. `restoreSession` now fetches `/auth/me` when the store holds a token but no user.
- **The operator's escape hatch was documented but missing.** `egress_policy` promised `scripts/egress_admin.py clear` for a downgraded tenant locked out of its own lockdown; it now exists (show / refusals / clear), audited with a reason, gated by D4 and MD18.
- **`sweep_process.py` was committed without its executable bit** (a clean b76347a checkout fails `verify_arch49` A1); the apply sets it — on Windows record it with `git add --chmod=+x` (§10).

## 10. Deploying

From the repository root, with `apply_arch50.py` and `verify_arch50.py` in `backend\` and `run_arch50.ps1` in the root: `.\run_arch50.ps1` (add `-AllowUnpriced` on a development database, `-SeedPriceBook` on a fresh one, `-CheckOnly` first if you like). It checks the tree, applies (and proves a second apply writes nothing), confirms `cryptography` and `openai` (already pinned), migrates to `arch50_step1_sovereign_revops` — never `head`; if the contract step already ran it stamps around it —, seeds an Enterprise tier version carrying `capability.egress_lockdown` (`--carry-forward` moves live subscriptions onto it), installs the console's packages and runs `verify_arch50.py --db --mutate --build --regression`. Then `.\run_arch50.ps1 -GA2` for the clean-database chain, and `git checkout -- backend/evidence` to restore the per-milestone evidence the suites rewrote. Before committing on Windows: `git add --chmod=+x backend/scripts/sweep_process.py backend/scripts/sweep_revops.py backend/scripts/dr_pitr.py backend/scripts/licence_tool.py backend/scripts/release_manifest.py backend/scripts/ga2.py`.

Optional, all operator settings in `backend\.env`: `EGRESS_MODE=deny` with `EGRESS_OPERATOR_HOSTS`; `LOCAL_LLM_MODE=fallback|exclusive`, `LOCAL_LLM_BASE_URL`, `LOCAL_LLM_MODEL`, `LOCAL_LLM_API_KEY`; `FLOWPILOT_EDITION=sovereign` with a licence (`scripts/licence_tool.py`, installed from the console or `LICENCE_FILE`); PITR: `archive_command = '<venv>\python <repo>\backend\scripts\dr_pitr.py archive-wal "%p" "%f"'`, `archive_timeout = 60`, `FLOWPILOT_WAL_ARCHIVE_DIR` on another disk or host, and the cron lines in `deploy/cron.d/flowpilot-sweepers` (revops daily, dr-heartbeat every minute, base-backup nightly, pitr-drill weekly). To take it back: `.\run_arch50.ps1 -Rollback -WithDatabase` (database first, then code).
